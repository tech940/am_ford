Three findings that are commercial, not cosmetic

The homepage search can return zero results on its default state. Each control validates against stock; the combination never does. New + $35–45k returns nothing, because the only sub-$45k unit is the Certified Escape.

The inventory hero form discards everything typed into it. Its Name and Phone inputs have no value, onChange, name or ref. The submit handler reads only the vehicle id, then opens a modal that asks for the same fields again.

A VIN is fabricated into every listing-page lead. vin: `1FT${id.toUpperCase()}2025` — in a file whose own type comment warns in capitals that a fabricated VIN is a legal risk.

Two published rates contradict each other and both contradict the site's own copy: cards print payments at 7.49% APR, /financing hardcodes 5.9% with no control to change it, and the inventory page asserts in two places that no rate or payment is published anywhere.

03Design direction: Ledger

On most dealer sites, photography and information fight inside the same rounded card. The organising idea here is that they never share a register.

Photography sells. Typography informs. Vehicles get full-bleed, wide, uncropped scale. Specification gets rules, a baseline grid, and tabular figures. Nothing tries to do both, which is what produces the badge-clutter the current cards suffer from — the Escape shows "Certified Pre-Owned" twice, the Lightning shows "Electric" and "EV" simultaneously.

Why this, for this business

AM Ford is a family-owned single-location store in a town of about 3,000, whose actual competitive weapon is removing distance as an objection. It is not a luxury marque and should not borrow that vocabulary. It is also not a dealer-template store, and should not look like one. Plain, physical, and confident without shouting is both truer and more distinctive than either default.

Type

Drop Inter. It is the default of every generated interface and gives this store no voice. Archivo and Archivo Expanded (SIL OFL, self-hosted) replace it — one superfamily, two optical widths. Archivo is American, broad, slightly mechanical; it reads automotive without wearing a costume. Expanded is reserved for display sizes only.

Colour

Every dealer template and nearly every generated site defaults to cold slate. Going warm is the cheapest and strongest differentiator available, and it costs nothing in contrast.

Every option derives from the lot

The lot is 6 vehicles — 5 New, 1 Certified Pre-Owned, all Ford, all automatic, $36,450–$71,990, 6–8,420 miles. Today the filter panel offers FWD (0 vehicles) and Manual (0), a price slider spanning $20k–$100k, and a mileage slider whose top 83% is dead track. The sitemap generator builds facet URLs from the same hand-written list, so the day the F-150 sells, ?type=Truck becomes a submitted, self-canonical, indexed empty page.

One helper — facetsFromLot(vehicles) — feeds every option list, slider bound, chip, and sitemap facet. A control that cannot be satisfied does not render.

Card anatomy

Wide photograph, full card width, no inset. Below the rule: year and model in Archivo 21px, trim in ink-2 on the same line, price right-aligned in tabular figures. A single spec row — odometer · fuel · drivetrain — at meta size. One condition chip. The whole card is one link; there is no second button competing with it.

Comparison

With six vehicles, comparison is the page's real job. A persistent compare tray holds up to three and opens a specification table — rows as attributes, columns as vehicles, differing values emphasised. Tables are what this content is; the current modal renders them as cards.

08Forms and modals

Eleven overlay surfaces, seven implementations, eight z-index tiers, five backdrop treatments, six close-button treatments. LeadCaptureModal sits at z-50 — below the nav sheet at z-60, so the navigation renders on top of an open lead modal.

Two components replace all of it

  1. <VehicleLeadDialog vehicle intent /> — every vehicle-scoped ask. vehicle is a required prop, which turns the current wrong-vehicle bug (related-card "Get Price" files a lead for the page's car, not the clicked one) into a compile error.
  2. <ContentConversion /> — mandatory last section of every non-primary route. One field, phone, with a route-derived lead type. Seventeen routes currently end in a flat row of navigation buttons and capture nothing; three state an explicit ask ("Bring us a listing and a VIN") with no control behind it.

Non-negotiables

  • Every field: persistent label bound by htmlFor, hint before the input, error in role="alert". Twenty controls have no accessible name today.
  • One consent posture. Three exist now — a checkbox, a passive paragraph that drops both "consent is not a condition of purchase" and the STOP opt-out, and none at all. LeadCaptureModal takes a phone number with no consent while promising information "will never be shared with third parties", as financing leads go to lenders.
  • Submission goes through one path, so the converted-visitor flag is unconditional. Today a $71k test-drive booking is followed by a $500 discount modal, because three surfaces bypass the helper that sets the flag.
  • CTAs name their outcome. Never Submit.

Chat widget

It presents a scripted bot as a live person — "You're chatting with AM Ford", a pulsing presence dot, a fake typing delay, no free-text input, no automated-assistant disclosure. It asserts "it's in stock as of today" with no lookup and offers to hold a vehicle through a mechanism that does not exist. Either disclose it as automated and strip every unverifiable claim, or remove it. I recommend removing it: it duplicates a phone number that a six-car store can answer.

12Decisions I need from you

I have taken defaults on everything else. These four change the work materially, and I would rather ask than build twice.

1 · Is the six-vehicle lot real, or placeholder for a live feed?

The single largest input. If it is real, the homepage shows the whole lot and inventory becomes a comparison surface — which is the plan above, and it is a genuinely better site than a 400-unit competitor's. If it is placeholder, inventory needs pagination, saved searches, and facets that scale, and the homepage shows a selection. Default I will take: real, degrading gracefully upward.

2 · Do the published rates and payments come out?

7.49% APR on cards, 5.9% hardcoded on /financing, an adjustable calculator down to 2.9%, and a trade estimate the code admits reads neither make nor model. PRODUCT.md prohibits all of it without sign-off, and the site's own copy says twice that it publishes none of it. Default: all of it comes out, and the desk supplies every figure.

3 · Does the chat widget stay?

It cannot stay as-is — it presents scripted replies as a person and asserts stock it never checks. Removing it is my recommendation for a store this size. Keeping it means disclosing it as automated and deleting the lender count, the approval promise, the trade statistic and the stock assertion. Default: remove.

4 · The $500 offer

It appears in four unreconciled forms — "exclusive", "one per customer", "cannot be combined" and "on top of your value" cannot all be true — and ExitIntentOffer says "your voucher is locked in" with no voucher, code, expiry or record anywhere in the system. If it is a real program, it becomes one object in lib/ with one wording and one set of terms. Default: one definition, or removed if it is not a real program.

Say go and I start at step 1. Nothing above touches the backend, the routes' URLs, the data layer, or the lead pipeline's destination.
